# Growth Corridor Radar: one-page explainer (for Jiya)

## 1. The problem (say this first)
HoABL sells plotted land fully online in Alibaug, Ayodhya, Vrindavan, Goa, Dapoli, Nagpur and Naigaon. The biggest decision upstream of every launch is **which corridor to enter next, and when**. Today that call is made from site visits, broker chatter and gut feel about "the airport is coming". I built a **decision-support tool** that turns public signals into a ranked, explainable, scenario-aware view of where land is likely to appreciate next.

## 2. Data sources (what's real, what isn't)
| Signal | Source | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Amenities, shops, schools, hospitals, rail stations, major-road km within 20 km | OpenStreetMap via Overpass API | **Real** |
| 19 catalyst projects (MTHL, Navi Mumbai airport, Mopa, Ayodhya airport, Ram Mandir, Samruddhi, Jewar, Vadhavan, bullet train, Virar–Alibaug corridor, NH-66, and others) | Project pages and press | **Real** (open years are my assumptions) |
| Tourism footfall | UP Tourism (Ayodhya 1.78 cr in 2017 → 16.44 cr in 2024; Mathura 7.79 → 9.01 cr), Goa Economic Survey (1.04 cr in 2024) | **Real** for 3 corridors, proxy for the rest |
| Night-lights growth | Stand-in for NASA Black Marble VIIRS | **Proxy** |
| Land price and appreciation history | Generated by a documented formula plus noise | **Synthetic** |

## 3. Features (11 per H3 cell per year)
The map is cut into **H3 hexagons** (Uber's grid, about 5 km² each): 2,821 cells around 13 corridors.
- **Catalyst index** = Σ project weight × e^(−distance/25 km) × phase(year). Phase is 0 before announcement, 0.35–0.7 while under construction (anticipation), 1.0 for three years after opening, then it decays as the effect gets priced in.
- Distance to the nearest catalyst, and **years until it opens**
- Tourism footfall growth, and night-lights growth (proxy)
- OSM density of amenities and shops, schools and hospitals, and major-road km
- Distance to a rail station, the town centre and the nearest metro

## 4. The model
- **Target:** 5-year land CAGR for windows starting 2014–2021, giving 22,568 rows. It is *synthetic*, because no public plot-level price history exists for these markets.
- **Hybrid model:** a Ridge regression for the trend, plus gradient boosting on what Ridge misses. I chose it because trees on their own can't extrapolate to a corridor they've never seen, and Ridge on its own misses interactions such as "an airport matters more when you're within commuting distance of a metro".
- **Uncertainty:** quantile boosters give P10 and P90. I then apply **conformal calibration (CQR)** so the 80% band really covers 80% on unseen corridors.
- **Explainability:** exact SHAP. Every corridor's prediction splits into "+8.0 pp from the catalyst index, +3.0 from tourism, −0.8 from distance to metro…"

## 5. Metrics (leave-one-corridor-out: each corridor is scored by a model that never saw it)
| | R² | MAE (pp/yr) |
|---|---|---|
| **Hybrid (used)** | **0.823** | **1.51** |
| Ridge only | 0.814 | 1.54 |
| Gradient boosting only | 0.806 | 1.59 |
P10–P90 coverage is 66% raw and 80% after CQR. The top drivers are the catalyst index, OSM amenity density and years until the nearest catalyst opens.

## 6. Limitations (say these before he asks)
1. **The target is synthetic**, so the model can only rediscover the formula that generated it. The metrics prove the *pipeline* works, not the market.
2. OSM is a present-day snapshot and is mapped unevenly (Naigaon has 12k points, Jewar has 23).
3. Night-lights are proxies, and infra dates slip all the time. That's why the scenarios exist.
4. There are only 13 corridors, so out-of-corridor validation is the honest test, and it's strict.

## 7. How it maps to Xanadu / HoABL
- **Corridor selection:** rank the next 20 candidate towns before spending on land acquisition or site research.
- **Launch timing:** the "years to open" feature captures the anticipation window. The best time to launch is 2–4 years before the catalyst opens.
- **Pricing and marketing:** the SHAP drivers become the sales story ("12 km from Mopa, opened 2023, Goa footfall +21%"). The P10–P90 range gives an honest appreciation claim.
- **Risk:** scenario toggles stress-test a launch against a delayed expressway or airport.
- **Plug-in point:** replace the synthetic target with Xanadu's own transaction or CRM data (sales velocity, enquiry-to-booking rate per micro-market) and the same pipeline retrains.

## 9. Likely CTO questions
1. **"Is this real price data?"** No, and I flag it on every screen. Public plot-level price history doesn't exist for these markets, so I generated a calibrated synthetic target to build and validate the full pipeline. The inputs (OSM, infra, tourism) are real. The first thing I'd do with Xanadu data is swap in real transactions.
2. **"Then what do your metrics prove?"** That the pipeline can recover a known signal from noisy, unevenly mapped real features under strict out-of-corridor validation, and that the uncertainty bands are calibrated. It is an engineering and methodology proof, not a market claim.
3. **"Why a hybrid instead of XGBoost?"** With 13 corridors, a pure tree model can't extrapolate to a new corridor whose features fall outside the training range. It actually lost to plain Ridge (0.806 vs 0.814). Ridge plus boosted residuals got the best of both (0.823).
4. **"Why leave-one-corridor-out and not random K-fold?"** Neighbouring cells from the same corridor are nearly identical, so random folds leak and inflate R². In practice the question is always about a *new* corridor, so that's what I test.
5. **"How do you know the uncertainty is right?"** Raw quantile models only covered 66% out-of-corridor. I applied conformalized quantile regression, which widens the band by an empirically measured amount, and brought it to 80%.
6. **"Why is Alibaug, HoABL's flagship, ranked low?"** Alibaug is already expensive (low affordability), OSM data there is sparse, and its next big catalyst, the Virar–Alibaug corridor, is assumed for 2032. Flip the fast-track scenario and it climbs two places (#10 → #8) with about +0.8 pp/yr. That's the point: a market can be great today and still not be the *next* one.
7. **"How would you get night-lights properly?"** NASA Black Marble VNP46A4 annual composites, aggregated per H3 cell. It needs an Earthdata login, so tonight it's a documented proxy. The steps are in `pipeline/README.md`.
8. **"How would this run in production at Xanadu?"** A scheduled job pulls OSM, VIIRS, infra news and CRM data into a feature store keyed by H3 cell and month, retrains monthly, and publishes JSON or an API to this front end. Drift checks run on feature distributions. The app is static, so it costs nothing to host.
9. **"What signals would you add next?"** Leading demand indicators (Google Trends, enquiry volume per corridor from the CRM, Priya's conversation intent), Sentinel-2 built-up change, registration counts, and circle-rate revisions.
10. **"What's the business decision this changes?"** Which corridor to research next and when to launch: 2–4 years before the catalyst opens, with an honest appreciation range for marketing and a stress test for delays.
